## 3. The constraint that changes the port design

**Exactly one stream device may be open per NHI while that cable's
`thunderbolt_net` netdev is up.** The NHI has three ring-hop slots: hop 0 is the
control channel, hop 1 is `thunderbolt_net`, hop 2 is the only one a stream can
take. Proven by making it fail on demand (`invalid hop: -1` in dmesg) and by two
independent derivations of `hop_count` from the interrupt mask register.

This kills the `fn0`/`fn1` unidirectional pair on one cable that §1/§3 of the
memo assume. See §5c of the memo for the three routes out and the recommendation
(measure whether the mutex contends at all before splitting across cables — the
split costs you cable independence and the bench cable).

## 4. Where the C-state penalty actually lives

Measured on rail0, mean ICMP RTT by inter-message gap, PM QoS 100:

| gap | 2 ms | 5 ms | 10 ms | 50 ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| mean | 0.087 ms | 0.112 | 0.143 | 0.150 |

The **minimum stays ~0.08 ms throughout** — the floor never moves, only the
share of samples paying a wake. A tight exchange never idles long enough to
reach C2. Decode gates are ~1069 µs apart (§14.1), inside the free region.

Consequence: `pmqosLatencyUs = 100` costs this transport nothing, and the
~60 W/box the POLL floor bought is pure saving. `isolcpus` is **not** indicated —
the APU shares a package power budget and §14.1 puts own-GPU-compute at ~88% of
the gate against exchange's ~11%, so trading GPU boost for a C-state exit on the
11% component is a wash at best. Pinning via `sched_setaffinity` needs no kernel
parameter if it is ever wanted.
